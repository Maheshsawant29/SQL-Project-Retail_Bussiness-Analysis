01 · EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A Complete Consulting-Grade Business Diagnostic

This project transforms a simulated Retail Optical Store's raw transactional database into an end-to-end analytics engine — covering customer lifecycle, referral economics, churn risk, staff performance, and product/brand strategy — to guide data-driven management decisions.

Business Problem

Management lacked visibility into where customers convert, disengage, and churn across the full lifecycle — making it impossible to prioritize retention, referral, or staffing investment with confidence.

Data Size

9 interconnected relational tables — Customers, Invoices, Invoice Items, Products, Product Types, Brands, Staff, Prescriptions & Referrals — covering 8,000 walk-in visitors and 3,721 completed transactions.

Tools Used

MySQL (advanced SQL: CTEs, Views, Window Functions, ROLLUP), Advanced Excel (Pivot Tables & Dashboards), GitHub for project/version management.

Key KPIs

46.51% conversion rate · 28.25% retention rate · 6.32% blended churn · 10.18% referral revenue contribution · ₹4.68 Cr total revenue.

Major Findings

Senior customers (60+) dominate every lifecycle stage; repeat customers are just 28% of buyers yet generate 53% of revenue; first-time buyer churn dwarfs repeat-customer churn.

Business Value

Identifies ₹47.8L of at-risk churned revenue, a zero-cost referral channel worth ₹47.6L, and a high-margin Progressive Lens opportunity — providing a prioritized roadmap for revenue growth.

02 · BUSINESS PROBLEM

Why Customer Lifecycle Analysis Matters

Although the store receives a significant number of walk-in visitors, not every visitor becomes a buyer, not every buyer returns for future purchases, and some loyal customers eventually stop visiting altogether. Without understanding where customers drop off during their journey, management cannot accurately identify weaknesses in customer acquisition, sales conversion, retention, or staff performance.

The success of a retail business is not determined solely by the number of customers visiting the store, but by how effectively those customers progress through each stage of the customer lifecycle. This project delivers a comprehensive customer lifecycle analysis to identify bottlenecks, improve retention, reduce churn, and maximize customer lifetime value.

06 · CHURN ANALYSIS

Where the Business Is Losing Customers

Acquiring a new customer is far more expensive than retaining an existing one. This module isolates exactly where, why, and how much revenue the business is losing.

Dashboard 5 — Churn Overview: First-Time vs Repeat Buyers

• Out of 3,721 buyers, 1,615 have churned overall (43.4% using the correct unweighted rate) — 1,380 (85%) are first-time buyers who never returned, and only 235 (15%) are lapsed repeat customers.

• First-time buyer churn (51.69%) is more than double repeat customer churn (22.36%) — the biggest leakage happens immediately after the first purchase, not after years of loyalty.

• Repeat customer churn is proportionally smaller but far costlier: repeat customers generate 53% of revenue, so losing even 22.36% of them is a disproportionate financial hit.

• Senior Customers post the highest churn volume in both categories (504 first-time + 85 repeat) — but this reflects their much larger population, not weaker loyalty; their repeat-churn rate (21.68%) is close to the overall average.

• Churn is evenly spread across vision types (Bifocal 47, Distance 65, Near 65, Progressive 58 repeat-churners) — no single prescription category drives disengagement.

• Naksh Gandhi, Pratyush Kata and Anita Vala carry the highest churned-customer counts (16, 15, 15 respectively) among staff, worth reviewing for follow-up quality.

• 235 churned customers generated ₹47.8 lakh (10.21% of total revenue) historically — a clearly quantified, recoverable revenue pool.

Strategic Recommendations:
  • Redesign the post-first-purchase journey: follow-up calls/WhatsApp, satisfaction surveys, loyalty enrollment immediately after purchase #1.
  • Build a 90–180 day "At Risk" monitoring workflow to intercept repeat customers before they fully churn (customers like Leela Ghosh at 25 days, or dozens sitting at 170–180 days, are still recoverable).
  • Run prescription-renewal reminder campaigns every 2–3 years targeted at Senior customers, whose natural presbyopia progression creates recurring demand.
  • Prioritize outreach to high-value churned customers first (e.g. Vedant Saxena — ₹47,956 lifetime revenue, 506 days inactive).
Business Impact: Converting even a modest share of first-time buyers into a second purchase compounds directly into the high-value repeat-customer pool that already drives 53% of revenue.

08 · REFERRAL ANALYSIS

The Zero-Cost Growth Channel

Dashboard 7 — Referrer & Referral Performance

• 721 customers (9.01%) acted as referrers, generating 810 referred visitors, of which 381 became buyers — a 47.04% referral conversion rate, nearly identical to the store's overall 46.51% conversion rate.

• Referral customers contributed ₹47.64 lakh — 10.18% of total revenue — from only ~10% of the customer base, at effectively zero marketing cost.

• Surprising finding: Non-buyer referrers (388, 48.85% conversion, ₹26.5L generated) perform almost identically to buyer referrers (333, 44.92% conversion, ₹21.1L generated) — advocacy is driven by trust and consultation experience, not purchase status.

• Senior Customers (60+) are the strongest referral engine across every metric: most referrers (258), most referrals given (288), highest referral revenue (₹18.4L / 39%), and they are also the most common recipients of referrals from every other age group — the central node of the referral network.

• Teenagers refer the fewest people (17 referrers) but post the highest referral-to-buyer conversion (~59%) and highest referral retention (~35.7%) — an underpenetrated, high-quality growth channel.

• Referral retention (29.66%) lags overall retention (28.08% new) only slightly, but still leaves room for improvement — only 113 of 381 referral buyers returned for a second purchase.

Strategic Recommendations:
  • Build a structured referral rewards program — the current 9% referrer rate could realistically expand to 15–20% with formal incentives.
  • Launch family referral campaigns specifically targeting Senior customers, who are both top referrers and the most-referred-to demographic.
  • Investigate why non-buyers refer at such a high rate via customer feedback — likely signals strong consultation experience independent of purchase.
  • Target school/college outreach and social campaigns to grow the small but highly efficient Teenage referral segment.
Business Impact: Referrals already deliver a ~10% revenue channel with no ad spend; scaling referrer participation is the single most cost-efficient growth lever available to the business.

16 · SQL QUERIES & VIEWS

Query Library — Problem → Code → Insight

A representative sample of the analytical SQL queries used throughout the project. Each is framed as a business question, backed by production SQL, and closed with the resulting insight.

Business Objective: Identify which age segment represents the largest share of walk-in visitors to guide marketing and inventory focus.

SELECT
    CASE
        WHEN age < 20 THEN "Teenage_Customer"
        WHEN age BETWEEN 20 AND 30 THEN "20's Customer"
        WHEN age BETWEEN 31 AND 40 THEN "30's Customer"
        WHEN age BETWEEN 41 AND 50 THEN "40's Customer"
        WHEN age BETWEEN 51 AND 60 THEN "50's Customer"
        WHEN age > 60 THEN "Old_Age Customers"
    END AS Age_group,
    COUNT(*) AS Count_of_Customers,
    ROUND(COUNT(*) * 100 / 8000, 2) AS Percentage
FROM customers_details
GROUP BY Age_group WITH ROLLUP
ORDER BY Count_of_Customers DESC;

Business Insight: Old Age Customers (60+) are the largest segment — 2,920 customers (36.5%) — followed by a fairly even split across the 20s–50s (14–17% each). Teenagers are the smallest segment at 2.9%.

Business Objective: Determine which age group converts walk-ins into buyers most effectively.

SELECT
  CASE
    WHEN age < 20 THEN "Teenage_Customer" ... END AS Age_group,
  ROUND(COUNT(DISTINCT i.customer_id)*100 /
        COUNT(DISTINCT c.customer_id), 2) AS Conversion_Rate
FROM customers_details AS c
LEFT JOIN invoices AS i ON c.customer_id = i.customer_id
GROUP BY Age_group
ORDER BY Conversion_Rate DESC;

Business Insight: Conversion is remarkably consistent (44%–48%) across every age group. 50's Customers convert best (48.18%), but Teenagers convert almost as well (48.03%) despite being the smallest visitor pool — a signal of untapped acquisition potential.

Business Objective: Create a single reusable ranking of the highest-value customers to power every downstream high-value-customer query.

CREATE VIEW top_50_customers AS
SELECT
    i.customer_id,
    SUM(i.final_bill_amount) AS Total_Revenue_by_Customer
FROM invoices AS i
GROUP BY i.customer_id
ORDER BY Total_Revenue_by_Customer DESC
LIMIT 50;

Business Insight: The Top 50 customers generate ₹23.71 lakh (4.64% of total revenue) from just 50 out of 3,721 buyers — and 100% of them are repeat customers, proving loyalty is the strongest predictor of high customer value.

Business Objective: Flag repeat customers who have not returned in 180+ days as "churned," reusable across age, staff and vision-type churn breakdowns.

CREATE VIEW churn_customers AS
SELECT
    rc.customer_id, c.customer_full_name, c.mobile, c.age,
    c.assigned_staff_id,
    DATE(MAX(i.invoice_date)) AS last_visited_date,
    DATEDIFF((SELECT MAX(invoice_date) FROM invoices),
              MAX(i.invoice_date)) AS Number_of_days_from_last_visit
FROM repeated_customers AS rc
INNER JOIN customers_details AS c ON rc.customer_id = c.customer_id
INNER JOIN invoices AS i ON rc.customer_id = i.customer_id
GROUP BY rc.customer_id
HAVING Number_of_days_from_last_visit > 180
ORDER BY Number_of_days_from_last_visit DESC;

Note: Because the dataset is historical, churn is calculated relative to the dataset's most recent invoice date (2026-01-11) rather than CURDATE(), avoiding overstated inactivity.

Business Insight: 235 of 1,051 repeat customers (22.36%) have churned, at risk of losing ₹47.8 lakh in historical revenue permanently.

Business Objective: Test whether only paying customers refer others, or whether non-buyers also drive word-of-mouth growth.

SELECT
  'Regular_Customers' AS Type_Customers,
  COUNT(DISTINCT c.customer_id) AS Visited_Customers,
  COUNT(DISTINCT i.customer_id) AS Buyer_Customers,
  ROUND(COUNT(DISTINCT i.customer_id)*100/COUNT(DISTINCT c.customer_id),2)
    AS Conversion_rate
FROM customers_details AS c
LEFT JOIN invoices AS i ON c.customer_id = i.customer_id
LEFT JOIN repeated_customers AS rc ON rc.customer_id = c.customer_id
WHERE c.referred_by_id IS NULL
UNION ALL
SELECT 'Referral_Customers', COUNT(DISTINCT c.customer_id),
  COUNT(DISTINCT i.customer_id),
  ROUND(COUNT(DISTINCT i.customer_id)*100/COUNT(DISTINCT c.customer_id),2)
FROM customers_details AS c
LEFT JOIN invoices AS i ON c.customer_id = i.customer_id
WHERE c.referred_by_id IS NOT NULL;

Business Insight: Non-buyer referrers (388) generate referrals at almost the same rate as buyer referrers (333) — 48.85% vs 44.92% conversion — proving advocacy is driven by overall trust/experience, not purchase status alone.

Business Objective: Build one unified table combining assigned customers, buyers, repeated customers, conversion rate and loyalty rate per staff member.

WITH customers AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT c.customer_id AS all_customers, c.assigned_staff_id
  FROM customers_details AS c
),
customers_staff AS (
  SELECT cc.all_customers, cc.assigned_staff_id, s.staff_full_name
  FROM staff AS s INNER JOIN customers AS cc
  ON cc.assigned_staff_id = s.staff_id
),
customers_invoices AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT i.customer_id AS buyer_customers FROM invoices AS i
)
SELECT
  csiwrc.assigned_staff_id, csiwrc.staff_full_name,
  COUNT(csiwrc.all_customers) AS Assigned_Customers,
  COUNT(csiwrc.buyer_customers) AS Buyer_Customers,
  COUNT(csiwrc.repeated_customers) AS Repeated_Customers,
  ROUND(COUNT(csiwrc.buyer_customers)*100/COUNT(csiwrc.all_customers),2)
    AS Customer_Conversion_Rate
FROM customers_staff_invoices_with_repeated_customers AS csiwrc
GROUP BY csiwrc.assigned_staff_id, csiwrc.staff_full_name;

Business Insight: Staff performance is tightly clustered (conversion 39.7%–50.5%, revenue ₹18.4L–₹26.8L) — the organization runs on standardized process rather than star performers.

SQL Views — Reusable Analytical Building Blocks

Five MySQL views sit underneath the query library above. Each was created once, then reused across dozens of downstream queries — turning repeated multi-table JOIN logic into a single, consistent, query-once source of truth.

Purpose: Flags every customer who has placed more than one invoice, so "is this a repeat customer?" never has to be recalculated from raw invoices again.

Why it was created: Repeat-customer status is the single most reused filter in the entire project — it underpins the Lifecycle, Churn, Staff and Product modules. Centralizing it in one view keeps that definition consistent everywhere it's used.

CREATE VIEW repeated_customers AS
SELECT
    customer_id,
    COUNT(invoice_id) AS total_invoices
FROM invoices
GROUP BY customer_id
HAVING COUNT(invoice_id) > 1;

Columns produced: customer_id, total_invoices.

Reconstructed directly from the view's documented definition in the project report ("stores all customers with more than 1 invoice") — the report describes this view narratively rather than with a code screenshot, but the SQL is the direct, unambiguous implementation of that description.

Purpose: Identifies every repeat customer who has not purchased in over 180 days, ranked by how long they've been inactive.

Why it was created: Churn among repeat customers is the costliest kind to lose (highest lifetime value), so this view was built as the single reusable source for every repeat-churn question — age-group churn, staff churn exposure, and revenue-at-risk.

CREATE VIEW churn_customers AS
SELECT
    rc.customer_id,
    c.customer_full_name,
    c.mobile,
    c.age,
    c.assigned_staff_id,
    DATE(MAX(i.invoice_date)) AS last_visited_date,
    DATEDIFF(
      (SELECT MAX(invoice_date) FROM invoices),
      MAX(i.invoice_date)
    ) AS Number_of_days_from_last_visit
FROM repeated_customers AS rc
INNER JOIN customers_details AS c ON rc.customer_id = c.customer_id
INNER JOIN invoices AS i ON rc.customer_id = i.customer_id
GROUP BY rc.customer_id
HAVING Number_of_days_from_last_visit > 180
ORDER BY Number_of_days_from_last_visit DESC;

Columns produced: customer_id, customer_full_name, mobile, age, assigned_staff_id, last_visited_date, Number_of_days_from_last_visit.

Note: inactivity is measured against the dataset's own most recent invoice date rather than CURDATE(), since the dataset is historical and using today's date would overstate churn.

Purpose: Isolates buyers who made exactly one purchase and never returned, past the 180-day inactivity threshold — the largest single churn segment in the business.

Why it was created: First-time buyer churn behaves completely differently from repeat-customer churn (different volumes, different causes, different fixes), so it needed its own dedicated, reusable definition rather than being mixed into the general churn view. It chains four CTEs to isolate exactly the customers who exist in customers_details but do not exist in repeated_customers.

CREATE VIEW non_repeated_churn_customers AS
WITH customers AS (
    SELECT DISTINCT customer_id FROM customers_details
),
customer_details AS (
    SELECT c.customer_id, cc.age, cc.mobile,
           cc.customer_type, cc.referred_by_id, cc.assigned_staff_id
    FROM customers AS c
    INNER JOIN customers_details AS cc ON c.customer_id = cc.customer_id
),
customers_with_repeated_customers AS (
    SELECT cd.customer_id, rc.customer_id AS repeated_customers,
           cd.age, cd.mobile, cd.customer_type,
           cd.referred_by_id, cd.assigned_staff_id
    FROM customer_details AS cd
    LEFT JOIN repeated_customers AS rc ON cd.customer_id = rc.customer_id
),
-- extracting_the_non_repeated_customers
extracting_the_non_repeated_customers AS (
    SELECT cwrc.customer_id, cwrc.repeated_customers,
           cwrc.age, cwrc.mobile, cwrc.customer_type,
           cwrc.referred_by_id, cwrc.assigned_staff_id
    FROM customers_with_repeated_customers AS cwrc
    WHERE cwrc.repeated_customers IS NULL
),
buyer_non_repeated_customer_details AS (
    SELECT enrc.customer_id, enrc.age, enrc.mobile,
           enrc.customer_type, enrc.referred_by_id, enrc.assigned_staff_id,
           i.invoice_id, i.invoice_date, i.total_amount_items,
           i.total_tax, i.final_bill_amount
    FROM extracting_the_non_repeated_customers AS enrc
    INNER JOIN invoices AS i ON enrc.customer_id = i.customer_id
)
SELECT
    bnrcd.customer_id, bnrcd.age, bnrcd.mobile,
    bnrcd.customer_type, bnrcd.referred_by_id, bnrcd.assigned_staff_id,
    DATE(MAX(bnrcd.invoice_date)) AS Last_visit_date,
    DATEDIFF(
      (SELECT MAX(invoice_date) FROM invoices),
      MAX(bnrcd.invoice_date)
    ) AS Number_of_days_from_last_visit
FROM buyer_non_repeated_customer_details AS bnrcd
GROUP BY bnrcd.customer_id, bnrcd.age, bnrcd.mobile,
         bnrcd.customer_type, bnrcd.referred_by_id, bnrcd.assigned_staff_id
HAVING Number_of_days_from_last_visit > 180
ORDER BY Number_of_days_from_last_visit DESC;

Columns produced: customer_id, age, mobile, customer_type, referred_by_id, assigned_staff_id, Last_visit_date, Number_of_days_from_last_visit.

Technique showcase: 4 chained CTEs · LEFT JOIN exclusive filter (WHERE ... IS NULL) · scalar subquery · GROUP BY + HAVING on a derived column.

Purpose: Ranks every customer by lifetime revenue and stores the top 50 as a reusable virtual table.

Why it was created: Instead of recalculating the revenue ranking inside every subsequent query, this view is built once and reused across the age-group, customer-type, vision-type, product, and at-risk-VIP analyses — keeping every downstream query short and consistent.

CREATE VIEW top_50_customers AS
SELECT
    i.customer_id,
    SUM(i.final_bill_amount) AS Total_Revenue_by_Customer
FROM invoices AS i
GROUP BY i.customer_id
ORDER BY Total_Revenue_by_Customer DESC
LIMIT 50;

Columns produced: customer_id, Total_Revenue_by_Customer.

Purpose: Consolidates customer demographics, vision type, assigned staff, repeat-customer status, repeat-churn status, Top-50 flag, invoice details and item-level product data into one centralized, analysis-ready table.

Why it was created: Every product question — product-wise sales, brand performance, category analysis, vision-type vs product preference, age-group preference, repeat/churn/Top-50 purchasing behaviour — needs the same nine-table join. Building it once as a view eliminates rewriting that join for every query and makes the entire product module modular and fast to extend.

CREATE VIEW Product_Analysis_data AS
WITH customers AS (
    SELECT DISTINCT customer_id FROM customers_details
),
customers_detail AS (
    SELECT c.customer_id, cd.age, cd.mobile, cd.customer_type,
           cd.assigned_staff_id, cd.referred_by_id
    FROM customers AS c
    INNER JOIN customers_details AS cd ON cd.customer_id = c.customer_id
),
cd_vision AS (
    SELECT cd.customer_id, cd.age, cd.mobile, cd.customer_type,
           cd.assigned_staff_id, cd.referred_by_id, p.vision_type
    FROM customers_detail AS cd
    INNER JOIN prescriptions AS p ON p.customer_id = cd.customer_id
),
cdv_staff AS (
    SELECT cdv.customer_id, cdv.age, cdv.mobile, cdv.customer_type,
           cdv.assigned_staff_id, cdv.referred_by_id,
           cdv.vision_type, s.staff_full_name
    FROM cd_vision AS cdv
    INNER JOIN staff AS s ON s.staff_id = cdv.assigned_staff_id
),
cdvs_repeated AS (
    SELECT cdvs.customer_id, cdvs.age, cdvs.mobile, cdvs.customer_type,
           cdvs.assigned_staff_id, cdvs.referred_by_id,
           cdvs.vision_type, cdvs.staff_full_name,
           rc.customer_id AS repeated_customers
    FROM cdv_staff AS cdvs
    LEFT JOIN repeated_customers AS rc ON rc.customer_id = cdvs.customer_id
),
cdvsr_churn AS (
    SELECT cdvsr.customer_id, cdvsr.age, cdvsr.mobile, cdvsr.customer_type,
           cdvsr.assigned_staff_id, cdvsr.referred_by_id,
           cdvsr.vision_type, cdvsr.staff_full_name, cdvsr.repeated_customers,
           rcc.customer_id AS repeated_churn_customers
    FROM cdvs_repeated AS cdvsr
    LEFT JOIN repeated_churn_customers AS rcc ON cdvsr.customer_id = rcc.customer_id
),
cdvsrc_top AS (
    SELECT cdvsrc.customer_id, cdvsrc.age, cdvsrc.mobile, cdvsrc.customer_type,
           cdvsrc.assigned_staff_id, cdvsrc.referred_by_id,
           cdvsrc.vision_type, cdvsrc.staff_full_name,
           cdvsrc.repeated_customers, cdvsrc.repeated_churn_customers,
           tc.customer_id AS top_50_customers
    FROM cdvsr_churn AS cdvsrc
    LEFT JOIN top_50_customers AS tc ON tc.customer_id = cdvsrc.customer_id
),
cdvsrct_invoices AS (
    SELECT cdvsrct.customer_id, cdvsrct.age, cdvsrct.mobile, cdvsrct.customer_type,
           cdvsrct.assigned_staff_id, cdvsrct.referred_by_id,
           cdvsrct.vision_type, cdvsrct.staff_full_name,
           cdvsrct.repeated_customers, cdvsrct.repeated_churn_customers,
           cdvsrct.top_50_customers, i.invoice_id
    FROM cdvsrc_top AS cdvsrct
    INNER JOIN invoices AS i ON i.customer_id = cdvsrct.customer_id
),
cdvsrcti_invoice_items AS (
    SELECT cdvsrcti.customer_id, cdvsrcti.age, cdvsrcti.mobile, cdvsrcti.customer_type,
           cdvsrcti.assigned_staff_id, cdvsrcti.referred_by_id,
           cdvsrcti.vision_type, cdvsrcti.staff_full_name,
           cdvsrcti.repeated_customers, cdvsrcti.repeated_churn_customers,
           cdvsrcti.top_50_customers, cdvsrcti.invoice_id,
           ii.item_id, ii.product_id, ii.brand_id, ii.product_type_id,
           ii.quantity, ii.final_product_price
    FROM cdvsrct_invoices AS cdvsrcti
    INNER JOIN invoice_items AS ii ON ii.invoice_id = cdvsrcti.invoice_id
)
SELECT
    cdvsrctii.customer_id, cdvsrctii.age, cdvsrctii.mobile, cdvsrctii.customer_type,
    cdvsrctii.assigned_staff_id, cdvsrctii.referred_by_id,
    cdvsrctii.vision_type, cdvsrctii.staff_full_name,
    cdvsrctii.repeated_customers, cdvsrctii.repeated_churn_customers,
    cdvsrctii.top_50_customers, cdvsrctii.invoice_id, cdvsrctii.item_id,
    cdvsrctii.product_id, cdvsrctii.brand_id, cdvsrctii.product_type_id,
    cdvsrctii.quantity, cdvsrctii.final_product_price
FROM cdvsrcti_invoice_items AS cdvsrctii;

Columns produced: 17 columns spanning customer demographics, vision type, assigned staff, loyalty/churn/VIP flags, and full item-level invoice data.

Technique showcase: 9 chained CTEs · four sequential LEFT JOINs layering in independent flags (repeat, repeat-churn, Top-50) · two INNER JOINs unrolling invoices down to line-item grain — the single most complex object in the project.
